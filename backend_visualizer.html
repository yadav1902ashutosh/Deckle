Concrete Example: How "Lord of the Mysteries" is Stored

See how real data sits in each table and links together cleanly across NeonDB PostgreSQL.

1. Table: users (id: 1)
username: "cuttlefish", email: "author@deckle.com", password_hash: "$2a$10$..."
2. Table: personas (id: 1, user_id: 1)
display_name: "Cuttlefish That Loves Diving", handle: "cuttlefish", streak_days: 14
3. Table: books (id: 10, persona_id: 1, genre_id: 3)
title: "Lord of the Mysteries", slug: "lord-of-the-mysteries", views: 245000, genre: "Cultivation & Mystery"
4. Table: volumes (book_id: 10)
• Volume 1 (id: 101): "Clown Arc", volume_number: 1
• Volume 2 (id: 102): "Faceless Arc", volume_number: 2
5. Table: chapters (book_id: 10)
• Chapter 1: "Crimson", chapter_number: 1, volume_id: 101 (Part of Clown Arc)
• Chapter 50: "Farewell", chapter_number: 50, volume_id: 101
• Chapter 51: "Backlund", chapter_number: 51, volume_id: 102 (Starts Faceless Arc)
✨ Continuous numbering (Ch 1 ➔ 51) ensures URLs never conflict, while volume_id gives readers beautiful story arc headers!
