Chapter Two: The Night Road

edit_note Fleeting Dreams • schedule Updated Yesterday • auto_stories 3,420 Words

The moment I saw the corpse, I turned pale with fright. My grandmother had always said that if you encounter a floating corpse, you must never go into the river, no matter what. And if you encounter a corpse that looks like it's surrendering and walking in the river, you have to get away from the riverbank immediately and run for your life!

Zhang Yidan followed my gaze and saw the strange corpse that had appeared next to Huang Dong at some point. He immediately yelled, "Damn it, are you stupid, Huang Dong! Get up here! Can't you see the corpse over there?!"

His friends were all terrified, and Huang Dong's face was also pale. However, his family lived near this river, and this wasn't the first time he had seen a floating corpse. Relying on his status as the leader of the class, Huang Dong straightened his shoulders and sneered back: "You cowardly Zhang Yidan, you say you're fat and brave, but why are you so afraid of death? Doesn't one or two float down this river every month?"

water
The Murky Depths of Kanglong River Cold mountain runoff masked the sudden string of bubbles rising where the boys were wading.

As soon as he finished speaking, he watched helplessly as his companion right beside him was pulled beneath the water as if he had evaporated into thin air. A violent cluster of pale froth burst onto the surface!

Huang Dong turned white as chalk. He opened his mouth wide to shriek for help when something iron-clad clamped onto his ankle. A monstrous, downward drag hauled him into the eddies. He was an agile swimmer who knew the river currents, but against that unnatural grip, thrashing availed nothing.

"Huang Dong!" Zhang Yidan, instinctively loyal, leaned toward the current to reach for his flailing hand.

I watched helplessly from the clay bank. The chilling prickle against the nape of my neck flared into an all-out alarm bell. "Yidan, don't throw your life away! Run! Go find an adult!" I clamped my hands onto Zhang Yidan’s shirt and hauled him backward. The dead thing’s hollow eye sockets had locked directly onto me. If we lingered another second, none of us would walk home.

"Do not attend my funeral. Do not return to Xiaoyitun village. Forget the people and the rituals buried in those mountains, and never come back, even in death."

The next morning, the school principal gathered us to report that Huang Dong and his companions had drowned. Adults whispered that when they dragged the poor boys out, their eyes were still wide open, gazing straight into the gray sky. One rescuer had even succumbed to cardiac arrest while hauling the ropes.

At that time, I kept questioning: could the phantom hand brushing my collar be the child bride my grandmother had betrothed to me in secret? Was she not living flesh, but an ancestral shade?

Hidden within her yellowed envelope lay that terrifying talisman—its vermilion characters darkened to rusty brown. I recognized the stroke patterns: a Spirit-Communicating Talisman, painted exclusively with blood from the middle finger. A craft designed to shorten one’s own vitality in exchange for tearing open communion with the unseen.

Now, stepped off the rattletrap minivan at Kanglong village, the mountain mist wrapped around my neck like cold silk. A grandfather leaning on a cane had warned me: "Young man, Xiaoyitun is only twenty hearths. In the old days, it was nothing more than an abandoned plague mortuary. No one walks that road past sundown."

*Shh, shh, shh.*

Behind me, soft brushwood crunched. As I halted, the footsteps ceased. When I took two strides forward, the phantom cadence resumed. Cutting through the shroud of gray fog, a tall silhouette stood motionless right in the center of the mud path...
