第二章：夜路

Chapter 2: The Night Road

drawTian Yun (浮梦流转) visibility428.1K Readers updateRevised Edition

The Kanglong River at the Third Watch: cold currents sweeping past ancestral stone pillars.

The wind off the Kanglong River carried the rancid smell of rotting marsh weed and damp clay. I tightened my grandfather’s worn hemp jacket around my collar, my boots sinking half an inch into the churning mud path. Not a single crickets’ cry dared echo from the reeds tonight; the silence was thick enough to curdle blood.

Before me, Zhang Yidan hurried his steps, his handheld bamboo lantern swinging like an erratic yellow eye across the flooded banks. "Jiang Tianyun, walk quicker!" he hissed under his ragged breath, not turning around. "My father said that before the rooster crows, whoever tarries near the third river sluice is asking to have their shoes taken. Once the drowned ones try your shoes on, you won't walk home with your own feet."

"Three drops of virgin blood upon yellow pith paper can deceive both heaven and river demons, but if the water touches the incense ash before the knot is severed, your soul sleeps with the silt." — From Grandmother’s Five Yin Codex

I pressed my right thumb hard against my index fingernail. Beneath the dried crust was fresh tenderness; barely an hour ago, Grandmother had forced my hand flat against the ancestral altar table, pricking the digit with an ox-bone needle to sketch the Spirit-Communicating Seal across my chest. Her fingers had been so icy they felt like tallow candles dug from winter soil.

"Stop," I whispered, freezing in my tracks.

Zhang Yidan stumbled, the bamboo lantern jerking violently. "What? Did you drop the copper coins?"

"Look at the water," I said.

Ten paces out from the embankment, the swirling brown surface had flattened into a circular mirror of oily black glass. Rising gently from the dead center of the whirlpool was not driftwood, nor the bloated carps usually trapped by the floodgate. It was the hem of an embroidered crimson silk skirt, buoyant and spreading wide like a poisonous lotus, swaying contrary to the river's rush.

sticky_note_2
Lore Context: The Red Bridal Ward

In local southern folklore, brides who met untimely watery deaths prior to marriage ceremonies form the most territorial class of river ghosts (*Shui Gui*). The scarlet weave repels ordinary willow branches.

The bride who had disappeared on the wedding boat three village crossings upriver was not floating toward the sea. She was coming against the current, right toward the Kanglong landing.

Zhang Yidan let out a strangled whimper. The bamboo lantern slipped from his trembling hands, extinguishing with a sharp hiss as it plunged into the shoreline slush. Darkness swallowed us whole, save for the faint, pale luminescence rising from beneath my hemp jacket—where grandmother’s vermilion seal was burning searing hot against my ribs.
